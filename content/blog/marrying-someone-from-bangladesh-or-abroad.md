It is one of the most common conversations in Bangladeshi families abroad. Parents suggest a match
from Bangladesh: a good family, well educated, recommended by relatives. Their son or daughter, who
has built a life in Toronto, London, New York or Sydney, is not sure.

Neither side is wrong. There are real advantages and real challenges either way. This guide lays
them out honestly, so families can discuss them together.

*This is general information, not legal or immigration advice. Rules change; always check official
government sources or speak to a licensed professional.*

## Marrying someone from Bangladesh

### What can work well

- **Shared roots and language.** Culture, food, family customs and expectations may align closely.
- **Family trust.** Families can meet, visit and verify each other through relatives.
- **A wider pool.** Your parents' network in Bangladesh may be far larger than your circle abroad.

### What can be hard

- **Time apart after the wedding.** Spousal immigration processes take time, and couples may live
  in different countries for months or longer. Check current information directly:
  [Canada](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/family-sponsorship/spouse-partner-children.html),
  [Australia](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/partner-offshore),
  [UK](https://www.gov.uk/uk-family-visa/partner-spouse),
  or the official immigration website of your country.
- **Adjusting to a new country.** The partner who moves leaves family, friends and often a career.
  Qualifications may not transfer easily. The first winters, or simply the distance from home, can feel long and lonely.
- **Different daily realities.** Someone raised abroad and someone raised in Bangladesh may share values
  but have different expectations about gender roles, work, and how involved extended family is.
- **Knowing each other well enough.** In a long-distance marriage search, matches often have limited time together before
  the wedding.

## Marrying someone already abroad

### What can work well

- **A shared daily life.** You both know what a long winter, a 9-to-5 commute, or raising kids
  far from grandparents is like.
- **No long separation.** You can meet in person, often, before deciding.
- **Similar experiences.** Growing up between two cultures, or rebuilding a life as an immigrant,
  is something you both understand.

### What can be hard

- **A smaller pool.** Outside the biggest cities, there may be few Bangladeshis your age who are
  looking.
- **Family expectations.** Parents may worry that someone raised abroad is "too Western", or that a
  family they don't know well is a risk.
- **Relocation within the diaspora.** Your match may be in another city or country, so someone may
  still have to move.

## Questions to talk through as a family

1. Where do we, as a couple, want to live long term?
2. Are we prepared for time apart after the wedding, if that is part of the plan?
3. How will the partner who moves rebuild their career and friendships?
4. How well do we know each other, beyond our families' recommendations?
5. What do both families expect about living arrangements and involvement?
6. Have we checked the official requirements and realistic timelines for our situation?

If that conversation is hard to start, see

## A word on intentions

Most people looking for a spouse abroad are sincere. But it is wise to be careful of anyone who
pushes to marry quickly, talks about visas before getting to know you, or asks for money. Our guide
to [online matrimony red flags](/blog/online-matrimony-safety-red-flags) has more.

## There is no single right answer

Some of the happiest couples met across continents. Others found each other in the same city. The
right choice is the one where both people go in with open eyes, honest conversations, and families
who support them.
